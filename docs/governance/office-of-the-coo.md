# Office of the Chief Operating Officer

**Project version:** 1.4.0 · **Release date:** 2026-11-02 · **Status:** Official release.

**Selected R14 target — Proposed, pending required approval.** No adoption, appointments or control tests are evidenced. Before cutover use the [current-authority reference](current-authority.md); a release grants no new powers.

## Purpose and boundaries

The Office of the COO is an executive working unit, not a board, a new approval committee or a portfolio hierarchy. The COO remains accountable for operations. Three Division Heads and the Technical Lead **report directly to COO**, never through Office staff. Office members report functionally to COO; a dedicated appointee has COO as permanent People Manager, while a concurrent holder retains exactly one home People Manager. Independent Oversight (OIO) remains outside operating command.

## Positions and appointment authority

| Appointment | Grade / qualification | Appointment and reporting | Owns / does not own |
|---|---|---|---|
| COO | 07 appointment-linked; organizational judgment, fair management, delegation, incident literacy and continuity assessment | Actual Owner appoints; reports to Owner | Operating priorities, four delivery-owner relationships, consequential appointments, exceptional tradeoffs, Council participation and Office accountability; no ownership transfer or OIO case command |
| Director of Operations Integration (Integration Director) | 05 minimum through assessed senior cross-functional work; mediation, capacity allocation, decision reasons, safety/conflict literacy and scenario exercise | COO appoints after relevant Heads and Personnel assessment; functional report to COO | Binding integration decisions only under registered mandate; no automatic grade 06, general Head supervision, technical certification, disciplinary power or acting COO status |
| Chief of Staff (CoS) | 05 minimum; records, prioritization, discretion, Council procedure and accessible communication | COO appoints; functional report to COO | Agendas, secretariat, executive records, commitment tracking, reviews and administrative follow-through; no operational arbitration, line command, disciplinary authority, vote or automatic succession |
| Operations Planning & Performance Lead (Planning Lead) | 04 minimum for accountable process ownership; demand/capacity analysis, uncertainty, privacy and non-quota indicators; 05 only for assessed senior scope | COO appoints after domain/Personnel assessment; functional report to COO | Analysis and planning process, not leave approval, case outcomes or other people's management decisions; conditional appointment |

No appointment is automatic because the chart contains it. Use fair work-relevant selection and [rank assessment](../personnel/ranks-and-appointments.md). Relevant specialist experience qualifies without moderation service. Working division leadership can hold a concurrent Office appointment only after capacity/conflict checks; independent OIO decision roles cannot concurrently hold operating Office mandates. Grade reflects valid appointment/assessment, not a universal command hierarchy.

## COO reserved work and distribution

COO translates strategy into operating priorities, supervises delivery owners, sets organizational capacity envelopes, makes reserved operating appointments and consequential management decisions, handles exceptions beyond delegated limits, votes on Council and ensures continuity. Heads own functional outcomes and routine decisions. Integration owns authorized residual coordination; CoS owns administration; Planning owns decision support. COO reviews exceptions/outcomes, not every delegated action. There is no serial Office signoff.

## Binding integration delegation

**R14-D must first be ratified; then COO issues a named mandate.** Appointment alone is insufficient. Default mandate validity is at most 90 days, renewable with workload/conflict review and explicit scope. No onward subdelegation unless the instrument names the recipient and limits; no scope growth by informal message.

| Domain | First decision owner | Integration Director's binding residual authority | Mandatory limits / consultation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scheduled session coverage | Game Head uses agreed qualified T&S coverage envelope | Choose between conflicting session/event commitments, reschedule or cancel within existing service priorities and accepted allocations | Consult Game/T&S and affected task leads; never conscript volunteers, certify enforcement or double-book responders |
| Shared maintenance window | Technical Lead selects safe technical options and routine window within agreed service limits | Choose among technically safe alternatives where Heads cannot agree service timing | Consult Technical Lead and affected Heads; cannot order unsafe deployment, grant access or approve restoration |
| Shared capacity/service conflict | Affected Heads agree bilaterally under existing allocation | Select which optional commitment pauses and allocate already-consented shared capacity inside COO-approved envelope | Consult affected owners; cannot override accepted-duty safety, home/host consent, staffing establishment, private records or fixed policy |
| Cross-division initiative | Named qualified Head/Driver decides within pre-agreed project charter | Decide unresolved sequence/dependency questions covered by mandate | No line authority over another Head, new permanent unit, financial commitment or new policy |
| Ownerless recurring dependency | Relevant Heads identify a consenting qualified functional owner | Assign a temporary deliverable owner and bounded decision scope within existing functions | Named acceptance, expiry and next review; no new permanent office or compulsory secondary assignment |

Consultation seeks expertise, not a second approval. If consultation is unavailable, use only the recorded safe option and known accepted capacity; otherwise reduce/defer service. Technical/safety owners can refuse an action outside qualification or safe limits, giving reasons; Integration cannot reverse that expert boundary. With no approved financial delegation, no spending, contracts or paid commitments may be authorized through this charter.

Every binding decision records domain/mandate ID, conflict check, consulted views or absence, options, reasons, selected owner/executor, resource limits, expiry/review and notification. Use one shared action entry; no confidential case attachments. Heads execute valid in-scope decisions without routine COO reapproval. An objection on scope, conflict or safety goes to an unconflicted COO/valid acting COO; pause the disputed unsafe/out-of-scope portion. Ordinary disagreement is recorded and can be reviewed without an automatic veto. Misconduct goes to OIO; protected appeal rights remain separate.

COO must decide matters outside the mandate: new organizational priority/capacity envelope, permanent establishment, consequential appointments, conflicting nondelegable obligations, enduring policy exceptions or unresolved expert safety boundaries. An urgent protective action uses existing responder authority, not invented Integration powers. Revocation is prospective, reasoned and notified by COO/valid acting authority; records and valid prior decisions survive. Revocation cannot suppress an independent case or retrospectively falsify a decision.

## Conflicts and absence

A concurrent Head cannot arbitrate a contested allocation favoring their own division, prior decision or close collaborator. Use a named qualified unconflicted domain alternate in the mandate; otherwise COO/valid acting COO or defer. The Office does not vote on the conflict. Pure execution of an already agreed allocation is not fresh arbitration.

Integration absence leaves domain owners' mandates intact. A named mandate alternate may act; CoS/Planning do not inherit arbitration. [Continuity](../operations/continuity.md) owns acting COO selection and activation; Integration is only a possible candidate. No personal-conflict recusal can be cured by appointing the conflicted executive's chosen proxy.

## CoS information and concurrent work

CoS may request the minimum nonconfidential status needed for a recorded executive commitment: owner, next action, blocker and agreed review date. Point to the existing record; do not demand another daily report or instant off-duty response. A Head can challenge scope/duplication to COO; CoS records a blocker rather than commands compliance or disciplines. Council cases and protected evidence are excluded. Missing an update is not automatically misconduct.

Concurrent appointments have one combined voluntary-capacity budget, an explicit primary job and Office allocation, one permanent home manager and a functional COO contact. The two authorities agree priorities before acceptance; conflicts use accepted-duty rules and a consenting reallocation, never two simultaneous compulsory shifts. Rescreen personnel/case access; Office membership grants none. If Office allocation displaces essential home work, reduce commitments, reassign the appointment or justify a dedicated post. When COO is unavailable, CoS continues records only within existing mandate.

## Planning, records and activation

Use qualified coverage, accepted-duty reliability with context, backlog age/effort, interruptions, recurring conflicts, anonymized retention themes and volunteered estimates of administrative effort. Case capacity comes only from OIO's non-identifying aggregate status; small counts that reveal people are withheld. No punishment-volume, hours-online, health-detail or popularity score. Planning does not build a new dashboard, surveil staff, decide leave or judge individual cases.

One exception/action register remains canonical. Heads maintain their existing entries; CoS tracks commitments; Planning reads the same permitted data. Brief asynchronous exception review replaces duplicate reports; no new standing meeting. Keep appointment/delegation/activation records restricted outside Git; publish only safe ownership and scope summaries.

At 20, combine coordination with qualified existing contributors; no dedicated Planning post. Narrow Integration mandates and unconflicted alternatives matter more than a title. At 30, activate substantive Integration work only if repeated unresolved dependencies justify accepted hours. At 60, separate Integration/CoS and conditional Planning when a four-week exception sample shows useful work, explicit displaced duties and qualified coverage. This is a review sample, not a volunteer activity quota. Review after 30/90 days and with changed availability; deactivate surplus appointments fairly. If no qualified coverage exists, reduce service rather than promise this Office is staffed.
