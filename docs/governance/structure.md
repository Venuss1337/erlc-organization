# Organizational structure and operating model

**Project version:** 1.3.0 · **Release date:** 2026-11-02 · **Status:** Official release.

**Selected target specification — Proposed, pending R13 ratification.** Until a recorded cutover, the pre-reform charter remains controlling; no vote or appointment is claimed.

## Selected organization

```text
Owner / CEO
├── COO
│   ├── Trust & Safety Division Head
│   ├── Game & Roleplay Operations Division Head
│   ├── Community & People Division Head
│   ├── Technical Lead — compact Technology Office
│   └── Chief of Staff — concurrent coordination appointment
└── Oversight Commissioner
    ├── IA investigators
    ├── Independent Appeals reviewers
    └── Assurance assessors
Council: collective policy relationship, never personnel command
```

Three divisions plus a compact office replace six directorates. Both permanent Executive Director appointments are retired, with no replacement portfolio office. COO has four delivery reports, plus a concurrent CoS function only when useful. Division Heads own budgets/capacity, functional standards and appointments within published delegation. They do not reapprove routine work. OIO remains outside COO command.

## Management design

A People Manager owns one person's permanent coaching and development. An operational lead directs an accepted session/incident/project, without automatically becoming a People Manager. A coordinator owns a deliverable without direct reports. The personnel record names exactly one permanent manager; secondary assignments never add another.

At 60, each large division has two working People Leads reporting to its Head; each Lead has up to nine contributors in the illustrative roster. Heads also deliver work. There is no Unit Manager layer between Head and People Lead. At 20, Heads supervise their small functions directly. At 30, Game Operations adds one working People Lead if its actual coaching load requires it. Technical Lead supervises two contributors at 60, justified by privileged technical mentoring and recovery control, not a separate Director title.

COO resolves cross-division tradeoffs directly. A named division head may drive a cross-functional project without supervising another Head; project scope expires on completion. CoS remains grade 05 minimum, directly accountable to COO, concurrent with existing work; no director command, extra vote, independent discipline or separate reporting form. Shared exception records replace duplicate briefings; no new recurring meeting.

Owner retains two principal executive reports: COO and Commissioner. Actual ownership cannot be transferred by this specification. Independent fact-finding, adjudication, appeal and assurance remain distinct.

## Alternatives and rationale

| Model | Integration / cost | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Six directorates plus optional two EDs | Six small mandates, potential portfolio relay; three-person Tech carries Director apparatus | Reject as target |
| Four consolidated directorates | Removes EDs, combines related work; still gives tiny Tech a full Director | Better, unnecessary technical grade/title |
| Three divisions plus Technology Office | Four direct delivery reports; compact technical authority; preserved specialist ownership | Selected |
| Three divisions with Tech buried in Community | Fewer COO reports, but weakens visibility of platform risk and overloads Community Head | Reject |

The merger risks broad Game and Community remits. Use working functional coordinators and measured service reductions, not hidden replacement directors. If four delivery reports exceed COO availability, reduce initiatives/delegate concrete decisions; a new management layer requires a new approved proposal.
