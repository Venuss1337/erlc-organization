## GOV-01 — interim five-seat Council

Retain five voting seats and equal votes. **Mode is determined by occupied appointments, not total headcount.** At 15–20 or 30 staff, inactive Executive Director seats are filled through the interim election below. At 60, use full Council only if the portfolios genuinely merit activation; an inactive layer is not filled merely to supply votes.

1. Owner and COO retain their seats; Staff Representative remains elected for six months under the current charter. No person holds two votes. OIO Commissioner/protected heads remain non-voting.
2. Each inactive Executive Director portfolio has one **interim portfolio delegate**. Prefer a qualified consenting functional Director in that portfolio; if none is available, a qualified functional coordinator with demonstrated cross-team judgment may stand. Grade is not a substitute for competence. Candidates cannot be CEO, COO, an Executive Director, an OIO protected-function head, or the Staff Representative; no new permanent position or grade results.
3. All non-executive organizational staff vote confidentially for each vacant portfolio seat, one vote per elector per seat; candidate profiles explain mandate, conflicts and availability. People & Culture administers with an unconflicted OIO process observer, or a screened outside election observer if P&C/OIO leadership is implicated. Publish eligibility, seven-day nomination and seven-day ballot windows. Most votes wins; tied candidates have a seven-day runoff. Abstention is allowed. Publish turnout and totals without identifying ballots. No turnout minimum manufactures votes; low participation is recorded as a legitimacy concern requiring renewed outreach, never Owner-appointed substitutes.
4. Elect up to two consenting alternates for each interim portfolio seat and for the Staff Representative using separate ranked-result ballots under the same one-vote/plurality/runoff rule. A person cannot simultaneously hold two seats or substitute in two seats. If conflicting winners occur, they choose one; reballot the other, never let leadership choose which votes count. Full Council portfolio seats may also have staff-elected **item substitutes** using this process. Substitutes confer voting authority only, not executive appointment, grade or operational powers.
5. Interim delegates serve until the relevant Executive Director actually takes office or six months, whichever is earlier. Review the need for the executive layer every 90 days under [activation criteria](../governance/structure.md#activation-and-direct-reporting). Transfer agenda/records when the Executive Director becomes seated. Alternates serve no longer than their election term and retain their normal home manager.
6. Quorum remains **four distinct unconflicted voters**, including at least one CEO/COO seat, at least one seated Executive Director or elected portfolio delegate/substitute, and the Staff Representative or their elected alternate. Ordinary policy needs **three yes**; major structure/constitutional/OIO charter needs **four yes**, plus actual Owner ratification where reserved. Denominator remains five; vacancies, abstentions and recusal never lower the threshold. Chair has no extra vote.
7. Personal conflicts remove the member from item discussion and vote except factual clarification. An elected unconflicted alternate substitutes for that item; minutes identify original member, conflict, substitute and vote. An alternate who also participated in the matter recuses too. Vacancy in elected seat triggers election for remainder of term; elected alternate covers meanwhile. A vacant COO seat needs an actual Owner-appointed acting COO with explicit Council-seat mandate; Owner seat designation follows the existing charter. No Owner/COO discretionary appointment of elected seats.
8. No eligible substitute or too few unconflicted voters means **no adoption**. Defer, recruit/elect alternates or obtain independent policy advice and reconvene. Neither independent adviser nor observer receives a vote. Genuine urgent safety measures use the existing COO seven-day power, with scope, reason, expiry, prompt notice and review; no constitutional amendment, representative removal or case outcome through that exception. Expiry cannot be reset by reissuing the same measure; continuation needs a valid Council decision or a separately reasoned actual Owner/platform protective exception under the existing charter.

A CEO/COO personal conflict is not cured by naming a proxy for that item; no discretionary replacement vote. If both executive seats are conflicted, the required executive quorum component is absent and the policy vote is deferred. Routine qualified safety containment remains possible; a conflicted executive cannot use urgent policy to benefit their own pending case or eliminate oversight.

**Bootstrap without fabricated ratification:** The existing charter explicitly requires the Owner to adopt an interim charter when ED offices are inactive. Owner may formally adopt this interim mode for **90 days**, following the notice/election safeguards above; that does not approve GOV-02–04. Once five seats are validly occupied, Council considers permanent GOV-01 by enhanced vote plus Owner ratification. If not approved by expiry, interim delegated votes expire and quorum may again be blocked; existing routine authority and narrow emergency powers continue. Record this risk before starting. If the existing full Council is available, use its current quorum/votes for permanent GOV-01 directly. A hostile or absent Owner can block reserved ratification; documentation cannot override that.

The interim adoption instrument must state electoral and challenge protections before its first ballot. During bootstrap, no new punitive representative-removal power or recall is activated by Owner-only adoption: resignation/term expiry are straightforward; a contested eligibility vacancy needs an independently reviewed **existing valid** staff-membership decision, with safe challenge and an elected alternate meanwhile. If no valid process exists, contested removal is blocked. The permanent removal/recall provisions below require permanent GOV-01 and applicable GOV-02 adoption. Do not use interim membership authority to adopt a sanction charter by implication.

### Representative protection and removal

Disagreement, voting against leadership, reporting abuse or low optional activity are never removal grounds. Voluntary resignation, loss of staff membership after independent due process, or a reasoned independent finding of serious representative misconduct may end the term. For misconduct, GOV-02 supplies investigation, independent external adjudication and independent appeal; COO cannot dismiss the representative. Council may record vacancy only after final qualifying determination, with the elected alternate substituting and ordinary affected members recused; four affirmative votes and Owner ratification required for a contested removal under this charter. Protective restrictions do not silently remove the vote. Staff may instead initiate a recall petition signed privately by 25% of eligible electors; removal requires a majority of valid recall ballots and participation by at least half the electorate. Use an unconflicted ballot administrator/observer and publish counts. A recall does not establish misconduct. Recall intimidation is an IA matter. No access to confidential ballots by the subject's manager.

## GOV-02 — protected oversight and reserved-office safeguards

Owner appoints Commissioner after a published role description and assessment of judgment, confidentiality, evidence/process literacy, conflict handling and ability to protect all three functions. Seek unconflicted IA/Appeals advice and Staff Representative procedural feedback; record selection reasons and conflicts without disclosing private applicant data. Six-month appointment reviews assess administration and resourcing, never demand desired case outcomes. Functional leads are appointed by Commissioner after relevant qualification checks and independent conflict advice; case discretion remains protected.

Commissioner removal for misconduct requires external fact-finding, Owner's reasoned initial decision and separate external appeal; managerial inability uses [non-disciplinary review](../personnel/management-and-development.md) with external process review. Owner may impose a narrowly necessary protective access restriction first, documented with reasons and the case-procedure review periods. Notify protected leads and Staff Representative of the procedural basis without case details; preserve restricted records before reassignment where safe. Owner must record any departure from these safeguards. The agreement constrains legitimate governance but cannot technically prevent Owner revocation or deletion.

### Owner-related review and non-cooperation

Before sensitive-case operation, IA lead, Appeals lead and elected Staff Representative jointly screen and select a consenting **three-person external reserve panel**, by at least two selectors' agreement after all unconflicted selectors are consulted. One selector cannot choose alone; replace a conflicted selector with an unconflicted Assurance assessor or elected representative alternate. If fewer than two unconflicted selectors or three qualified outsiders are available, record the panel as unavailable. Owner is invited to disclose conflicts and give a response, but cannot veto the panel because they are its subject. Selection creates no server ownership, legal authority or compulsory access.

Panel members must be independent of the alleged events and current reporting lines, trained for their assigned function, willing to preserve confidentiality and willing to state limitations. For each Owner-related case, assign one external investigator, another advisory initial adjudicator, and a third independent appeal reviewer; they do not jointly decide all three stages. An additional external assessor is needed if systemic assurance of their own disputed work is required. Use the same notice, response, evidence and appeal safeguards. No compelled production, subpoenas, account takeover or forced transfer of ownership.

Reserve-panel consent and eligibility expire after six months unless the unconflicted selectors revalidate them. Individual case access still expires after 30 days unless renewed with reasons under the case procedure. A selected reserve is not continuously on call and may decline; replace through the same selection checks, never with an unqualified available friend.

Owner may accept or refuse recommended remedies; publish a minimal privacy-safe summary of process, recommendation and acceptance/refusal after response and appeal opportunity, subject to safety and confidentiality. Functional leads may notify staff of verified obstruction or unavailable safeguards without revealing identities/evidence, through available authorized communication channels. They may decline new sensitive case intake beyond safe referral/preservation, and staff may step back or leave without retaliation. No covert copying of private evidence into personal archives or unauthorized alternative server. If Owner removes channels or access, document only what can lawfully and safely be retained, report platform violations directly, and state that internal accountability is blocked. External review is not a technical cure for hostile ownership.

## Rationale and tradeoffs

An all-Owner-appointed replacement Council would be simple but undermine representation; a reduced three-seat Council concentrates executive power and makes major votes fragile. Elected temporary portfolio delegates retain five-seat checks and existing thresholds, at the cost of elections and a possible startup delay. Three distinct external case roles cost real volunteer capacity; advisory-only Owner remedies are an honest limitation rather than invented independence. Case templates and workload reduction replace extra permanent committees.

No permanent units, ranks or portfolios are added or removed. The alternative of activating unnecessary Executive Directors purely for quorum is rejected. Review these changes 90 days after actual activation using case delays, conflicted assignments, coverage failures and decision backlog—not an improved subjective audit score.
