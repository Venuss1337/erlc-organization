# Operating practices, staffing and implementation

**Project version:** 1.2.0 · **Release date:** 2026-11-02 · **Status:** Official release.

Canonical home for operating cadence, staffing scale and implementation phases. Cadence entries schedule the [Staff Experience](../directorates/people-and-culture.md), [Player Voice](../directorates/community-experience.md) and [oversight](../oversight/independent-oversight.md) processes; those linked documents own their safeguards.

<a id="performance-accountability-and-meeting-rhythm"></a>
## Performance, accountability and meeting rhythm

<a id="daily-teams-operate"></a>
### Daily: teams operate

- Session squads handle assigned shifts and record concise outcomes.
- Moderators enforce their rules with sufficient case notes.
- Support handles ordinary requests and routes specialty cases.
- Managers resolve routine resource conflicts before escalating them.

<a id="weekly-managers-coordinate"></a>
### Weekly: managers coordinate

- Team Leads: short update on demand, blockers, staffing and quality.
- Directors: consolidate only material exceptions, risks and improvement actions.
- Chief of Staff: maintains organization-wide issue/decision log and asks owners of overdue tasks for updates.
- Workforce Coordinator: checks uncovered shifts and excessive secondary assignments.
- Staff Experience Coordinator: follows up on consented workload/leave concerns and optional mentor support without collecting intrusive personal details.
- Player Feedback Coordinator: routes public suggestions to one owning team and updates overdue tracker items.

<a id="monthly-governance-council-policy-review"></a>
### Monthly: Governance Council policy review

- Review pending cross-directorate proposals, expiring temporary measures, policy implementation blockers, staff-representative feedback and relevant **aggregated player consultations**.
- Revisit approved policies at their scheduled review dates; involve relevant independent oversight advisers without giving them operational control.
- Chief of Staff publishes non-sensitive decisions and tracks who is implementing each approved measure.

<a id="monthly-executive-performance-review"></a>
### Monthly: executive performance review

The COO and executive directors examine:

- Session reliability, player satisfaction and coverage.
- Enforcement quality, fairness and timely resolution — **not punishment count**.
- Recruitment readiness, **voluntary retention, training completion, workload concerns and staff experience trends** without individual activity quotas.
- Ticket demand, support quality, **player feedback themes, published improvement-tracker updates and consultation outcomes**.
- Availability of essential bots/systems, access risks and incidents.
- Open audit recommendations and independent oversight summaries that can lawfully/appropriately be shared.

<a id="quarterly-staff-experience-and-community-voice"></a>
### Quarterly: staff experience and community voice

- People & Culture summarizes voluntary staff pulse feedback, retention/leave themes and the workload changes made in response, without identifying individuals in small teams.
- Community Experience runs a brief player pulse survey and publishes a `You said / We did / Why not` summary; suggestions remain traceable to an owning team without publishing private complainants.
- The COO reviews barriers that operational teams cannot resolve; organization-wide policy gaps go to the Governance Council rather than creating another executive working group.

<a id="periodic-independent-review"></a>
### Periodic independent review

- **OIO / IA:** anonymized misconduct trends and process timeliness where feasible; no disclosure of active case identities.
- **OIO / Appeals:** overturns, errors and systemic fairness issues, without treating appeal reversal as a score to maximize/minimize.
- **OIO / Assurance:** sampled procedures, access control, training consistency, overlapping quality functions and root causes of significant failures. An unconflicted assessor checks OIO's own processes when necessary.

All performance metrics are diagnostic, not simplistic quotas. A staff member should not be rewarded for maximizing bans, ticket closures, or convictions.

## How small or large units should grow

The organizational unit is **a responsibility**, not necessarily a team of four people.

| Actual headcount | Appropriate structure | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Coordinator/single specialist reports to director or manager | Inventing a manager and deputy above a solo contributor |
| **2–4** | Team Lead or Coordinator with contributors, depending on workload | Creating middle management for prestige |
| **5–8** | Stable team with Lead, named alternate, clear rotation | Team Lead supervising every task personally |
| **9–15** | Consider multiple shifts or squads, each with a Lead | One huge always-on roster without coverage ownership |
| **Several squads** | Unit/Operations Manager supervising Team Leads | Additional directors for one function |

**Split a team** when shift coverage, task specialization, workload or supervision quality requires it; do not split just to produce an impressive org chart. **Merge teams** when their work is infrequent, overlapping and better served by one accountable lead. All headcounts above are illustrative heuristics, not strict thresholds.

<a id="example-60-person-staffing-concept"></a>
### Example 60-person staffing concept

A possible planning allocation, **not a universal target**: Trust & Safety 15; Live Operations 15; Roleplay Programs 10; Community Experience 6; People & Culture 4; Technology & Systems 3; Office of Independent Oversight 3 (Commissioner + IA + Appeals, with limited Assurance dual-hat and independent alternates); Executive Office 4 = **60**. The four executive positions are the COO, two Executive Directors and Chief of Staff. The Owner/CEO is counted separately from this illustrative **60 organizational staff** allocation; if the Owner is also counted as staff, adjust the totals accordingly. The Council reuses the COO and both Executive Directors, the Chief of Staff serves as a non-voting secretary, and the elected Staff Representative occupies an **existing staff seat** in one of the listed teams—**no five-person Council headcount is added**. Counts include leadership positions and are unique persons, not duplicate assignment slots. If people hold multiple team assignments, a team's seat count and organization-wide headcount must be reported separately. **Player Voice & Improvements** and **Staff Experience & Retention** are initially responsibilities of existing Community Experience and People & Culture staff, not extra persons in this illustration. Add a standalone OIO Assurance specialist only when justified by the workload and independence requirements, and rebalance the 60-person distribution accordingly.

**Growth trigger for executive layer:** Assess each portfolio separately under the [activation criteria](../governance/structure.md#activation-and-direct-reporting). An inactive portfolio reports directly to COO; Council seats require the applicable formally adopted charter. Do not fill appointments merely to match the chart.

## Capacity and service levels

**v1.1 procedure specification — GOV-03/GOV-04 adoption not verified.** Capacity means qualified people who consent to the actual accepted work window with usable access, not total roster or assumed weekly hours. Owning Directors decide reductions within existing scope; Council is needed for enduring policy changes, not each cancelled session.

| Service | Safe coverage check before accepting work | Degraded mode / stop trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Live ER:LC session | Named qualified Commander plus a **different available qualified enforcement responder**, required hosting/control access, safety/report contact and agreed end/handoff. Controller/extra host added if commander cannot safely manage session workload | One failed capability check: do not launch or end/pause safely. Reduce concurrent sessions, consolidate squads or lower admitted activity within session rules. One person cannot cover two simultaneous sessions as their only responder. |
| Support / events / RP activities | Named available owner, clear scope and referral route; live events with session risk meet session checks | Publish reduced hours/next update, pause optional events and slow nonurgent ticket work. No promise of continuous coverage. |
| Independent cases | Unconflicted investigator where required, separate adjudicator and credible independent appeal route; safe records | Use [case-capacity triage](../oversight/case-handling.md#triage-capacity-and-service-targets). Log blocked cases, seek alternates, review protective restrictions; never use conflicted review to clear backlog. |
| Critical technical service | Qualified maintainer with actual recovery authority, known safe degraded state and tested recovery path | Emergency containment only if backup/recovery missing; defer optional changes, keep unsafe systems offline. |

Before publishing an accepted duty window, the service lead records capabilities, consenting primary and backup if available, fallback and handoff. A backup is useful only if qualified, contactable and consenting; no roster placeholder counts as coverage. Volunteers may withdraw availability without private explanation. They should notify the lead when able; the lead arranges handoff or cancels service, rather than compel continuation. Repeated missed **accepted** responsibilities use readiness/coaching review, not automatic IA.

The separate commander/responder coverage is a local risk-control choice: the person maintaining session coordination should not simultaneously be the sole person handling enforcement and its evidence. It is not a research-derived staffing ratio. Cross-trained staff can hold either role in different windows; qualification and accepted availability, not nominal headcount, control the check.

Workforce Coordinator checks assignment overlaps against the [primary/secondary priority rule](../personnel/assignments.md#priorities-when-assignments-collide). Neither secondary leads nor executives can silently expand allocations. A measurable trigger is an uncovered required capability, two commitments overlapping for the same indispensable person, demand exceeding accepted capacity before next review, or a missed case/response target without a plan. Service owner reduces commitments immediately where necessary and records exception; COO resolves cross-service tradeoffs, not a new compulsory workload allocation.

Use only volunteered duty windows, counts of accepted/uncovered work, backlog age, estimated remaining work and qualification gaps. No mandatory hours, message counts, punishment targets or health details. Recruitment prioritizes the scarce capability/time window before extra managers. Director and P&C ask whether work can be stopped or simplified before recruiting. Leave and return keep the [Staff Experience safeguards](../directorates/people-and-culture.md#staff-experience--retention-framework).

These are planning examples, not appointments or proven coverage. Counts include Owner and each person once; RP players are excluded. Dual assignments never multiply headcount.

| Unique people | Example allocation (sums to stated total) | Realistic service / governance constraint |

One staffed session window may be feasible, continuous service is not. Qualified external/reserve case officers and technical backup still required. | 30 | Owner 1, COO 1, T&S 8, Live Ops 7, RP 3, Community 3, P&C 2, Tech 2, OIO 3 | Add dispatch/training capacity before more executive layers. A second concurrent session needs its own verified capability coverage. Same interim Council route if EDs inactive; no automatic layer at 30. |
| 60 | Owner 1, Executive Office 4, T&S 15, Live Ops 15, RP 9, Community 6, P&C 4, Tech 3, OIO 3 | Adjusts the original illustration to include Owner. EDs/CoS only if workload criteria met. ## Records, updates and meetings

Reading guide to existing requirements and the pending GOV-03 shared-register specification below. It does not cancel scheduled reviews or ratify a new reporting policy. The cadence above assigns checks/responsibilities; it is not a requirement for a separate document from every named role.

| Information type | Keep / communicate | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Required operational record | Existing roster, qualification, enforcement note, vacancy/assignment or decision record at its canonical location | Another copy just because a second manager is informed |
| Exception update | Changed demand, uncovered duty, risk, blocked decision, owner and next action; link the existing item where shared-register use is adopted | Rewriting the same item for Lead, Director, ED and CoS |
| Optional support/feedback | Consented check-in, pulse response, exit feedback or voluntary mentoring | Turning optional feedback into an activity obligation; collecting diagnoses |
| Scheduled review | Use existing evidence for the applicable weekly/monthly/quarterly or appointment check; capture actual decision/action | A new presentation or synchronous meeting without a decision need |
| Emergency notification | Contact relevant qualified responders/leadership promptly, with scoped safe facts | Waiting for a report cycle or publishing confidential evidence in the general log |
| Protected case record | Keep required procedural/evidence records in authorized restricted storage with case-specific access | Combining private IA, appeal or wellbeing files into the public/operational register |

P&C checks and Technology execution are not automatically extra substantive selection approvals. Council minutes must still record actual consultation, conflicts, quorum and votes; async deliberation is already allowed. Sensitive case notifications and response rights are not optional status reporting. Reuse a record/reference where it already provides the required information; retained control checks still happen. No new recurring report, meeting or certification is introduced by this guide.

## One action and exception register

**GOV-03 specification.** Use one shared operational record: item, decision/action owner, next step, due/review date, status, capacity/risk and decision reference. No confidential case identities, wellbeing details, credentials or private evidence in this general register. Case owners provide aggregate capacity/blocker information only.

Weekly team checks update **changed exceptions** in that record; Directors add only unresolved cross-team issues. Chief of Staff links to the same entry, never asks staff to rewrite it into another briefing. Their COO briefing is a filtered view, not another approval stage. Overdue work prompts a capacity/priority discussion, not automatic discipline. Routine delegated work has one accountable decision owner; no signoff from Director, ED and CoS merely because all exist.

Monthly Council/executive reviews may be asynchronous using this register and aggregate evidence. Hold a meeting only where a substantive decision/discussion needs it; valid Council notice, consultation and votes still apply. Quarterly staff/player feedback remains brief and voluntary; do not create duplicate surveys for each management layer. A blank or unchanged update is not mandatory busywork. Review any mandatory report after 90 days: retain only if a named recipient actually uses it to decide or act. Confidential and public improvement trackers remain appropriately separate; link item IDs rather than copy sensitive content.

<a id="implementation-phases"></a>
## Implementation phases

<a id="phase-1--foundational-governance"></a>
### Phase 1 — Foundational governance

- Ratify six directorate mandates, **one Office of Independent Oversight charter with three protected functional mandates**, and the Governance Council charter (including voting thresholds, reserved powers and member recusals).
- Seat actual CEO/Owner and COO appointments, elect a Staff Representative, and use the full Council only when Executive Directors are active. Otherwise formally adopt/elect the [interim Council](../proposals/v1-1-ratification.md#gov-01--interim-five-seat-council) before policy votes. A consenting non-voting secretary may keep records when Chief of Staff is inactive.
- Establish the Council proposal/vote register and publish the first schedule of organization-wide policies.
- Assign an Owner, operations executive/COO, and interim directors as capacity allows.
- Publish the eight organizational grades and job-to-grade matrix ([rank and eligibility framework](../personnel/ranks-and-appointments.md)), including top-grade appointment conditions, non-moderation routes and reversion rules.
- Publish a minimal authority matrix and urgent incident procedure.
- Separate technical permissions from job titles.
- Appoint an Oversight Commissioner and identify qualified IA, Appeals and Assurance leads/alternates; publish recusal, adjudication and referral rules before accepting sensitive cases.
- Assign one existing Community Experience owner to Player Voice and publish a safe non-sensitive improvement tracker.

<a id="phase-2--personnel-registry-and-assignments"></a>
### Phase 2 — Personnel registry and assignments

- Record each person's one organizational grade, grade basis, retained prior grade where relevant, Home Team, People Manager, qualifications and assignment slots.
- Appoint a Workforce / Assignment Coordinator.
- Define team-specific applications, role grade thresholds, grade-promotion approvals and an internal secondment request.
- Train leads on cross-team task authority and conflict resolution.
- Assign a Staff Experience Coordinator within People & Culture, launch opt-in 30/90-day check-ins, leave/re-entry options and a quarterly aggregate pulse review.
- Implement OIO's separate case access/registers and single-intake triage; train Case Operations, Session Quality and RP Standards on the referral matrix.

<a id="phase-3--scale-operations"></a>
### Phase 3 — Scale operations

- Create Session Operations squads based on coverage.
- Expand Game Enforcement and Member Services based on actual demand.
- Add specialist OIO reviewers/assessors where caseload or independence justifies additional unique staff; do not appoint extra heads merely for titles.
- Run a monthly player consultation and publish a `You said / We did / Why not` update; use quarterly player and staff pulses as feedback, not popularity contests.
- Activate the Executive Directors (grade 06) and a Chief of Staff (grade 05 minimum) once ongoing coordination work exists; do not create extra roles just to fill rank levels.

<a id="phase-4--continuous-assurance"></a>
### Phase 4 — Continuous assurance

- Run periodic Governance Council policy reviews without giving the Council authority over individual IA or appeal outcomes.
- Run OIO practical audits and anonymized Appeals/IA trend reviews, including a conflict-safe review of OIO's own process.
- Review staff retention and voluntary workload signals and publicly report improvements delivered from player feedback without disclosing personal case data.
- Check management spans, vacant coverage and unnecessary managerial layers.
- Retire redundant positions, merge low-volume functions, and improve SOPs based on real problems.
